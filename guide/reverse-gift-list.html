The reverse gift list: how to keep gift ideas all year

A wishlist is what you want. A reverse gift list is what the people you love want, written down by you the moment they say it.

By Little Bird · Updated October 18, 2026 · 7 minute read

In July your sister mentions that her headphones only work in the left ear. In October a friend picks up a speckled mug at a market, turns it over, puts it back, and says she’d use it every day. By December you remember neither. It’s the 22nd, and you’re holding a candle you don’t feel strongly about.

A reverse gift list fixes that. It’s a private, running list of the things people say they’d like, kept by person, all year, and it’s the most reliable way we know to keep track of gift ideas. Apartment Therapy gave it the name.

Reddit has recommended the habit for years. A 2017 r/LifeProTips post telling people to keep a note of gift ideas when someone mentions them has 6,085 upvotes, and the same tip picked up 1,353 more in April 2026.

This guide covers what to write down, what counts as a hint, where to keep the list, and a checklist you can copy or print.

What is a reverse gift list?

A reverse gift list is a private list of gift ideas for other people, built from things they mention during the year. You write it, you keep it to yourself, and you add to it whenever someone drops a hint: at dinner, in a group chat, or while complaining about a kettle that takes ten minutes to boil.

A wishlist works the other way round. The person who wants the gifts writes it and shares it, and everyone shops from it. Wishlists are efficient, and they take the surprise out. A reverse gift list keeps the surprise and still gets the gift right, because the idea came from them.

It works because the presents people remember tend to be the ones that prove you were listening. Handing someone the thing they mentioned once, months ago, says more than the thing itself. The list also moves the hardest part of gifting, thinking of something, out of December. When the season arrives, you choose from ideas you already have.

How to keep track of gift ideas, step by step

Seven habits separate a list you use from a note you forgot you had.

  1. Choose one place you can reach in five seconds

    Hints come up mid-conversation, and you have about as long as it takes them to change the subject. If saving an idea means opening a laptop or finding a notebook, it won’t happen. Your phone is usually the answer. Keep one list. Three apps and a pocketful of receipts add up to no list at all.

  2. Write down what they said, in their words

    “Mine just broke” and “I’d love one someday” point to different gifts. The first wants a good replacement soon. The second can wait for a birthday and be a little extravagant. Their exact words also remind you why it mattered, which helps when you write the card. Add the date and where you were.

  3. Add the details you’ll need at checkout

    “Blue sweater” is a guess. “The navy crewneck from the shop on Fifth, women’s medium, she said the green was too bright” is a gift. Save the size, color, model, a link, the price and where they saw it. A photo or screenshot is often faster than typing.

  4. Keep a “not for them” list

    Allergies, things they already own, the scented candles they quietly regift, the fortieth mug. A short line of no’s saves you from well-meant mistakes.

  5. Put the date next to the name

    A list that doesn’t know when anyone’s birthday is gets opened the night before. Add birthdays, anniversaries and the holidays you shop for, and set a reminder two weeks ahead. Two weeks is enough to order, ship and wrap without paying for overnight delivery.

  6. Mark what you buy, and log what you give

    Move each idea along as it happens: idea, bought, wrapped, given. That stops you buying the same thing twice in November. Keep what you gave by year, too, so last Christmas’s scarf doesn’t turn into this Christmas’s scarf.

  7. Tidy it once a season

    People buy things for themselves. Cross off what they already got, what sold out, and the hobby that faded in March. A shorter, current list is easier to trust.

What counts as a hint

Most hints don’t sound like requests. Listen for these.

“Mine just broke.”A better replacement, ideally before they get around to buying one.
“I’ve always wanted to try…”An experience: a class, a lesson, a tasting, two tickets.
“I keep losing my…”The small, useful upgrade they would never buy themselves.
“Look how cute this is.”The screenshot, the link, the shop window they stopped at. The most direct hint there is.
“Can I borrow yours again?”Their own one, so they can stop asking.
“That’s too much for me.”A splurge to save for a big birthday.

Where to keep your reverse gift list

Any of these works if you use it. Each has a weak spot.

The Notes appFast, free and already on your phone. It doesn’t know anyone’s birthday, can’t total a budget, and by year two it’s a long scroll of “mug??” and screenshots.
A spreadsheetSortable and good at budgets. Slow to open mid-conversation, and nobody edits a spreadsheet at a café table.
A paper notebookPleasant to write in and easy to flip through. It’s rarely with you when the hint lands, and it’s easy for the people in it to find.
A gift idea appBuilt for this, with dates, reminders and gift history. It’s one more app, so pick one that is private and quick to save to.

Questions

Is a reverse gift list the same as a wishlist?

No. A wishlist is written by the person receiving gifts and shared with others. A reverse gift list is written by the giver, from things the other person mentioned, and kept private.

How do I keep track of gift ideas for many people?

Give each person their own section with their date, sizes and a “not for them” line, and save hints the moment you hear them. Above five or six people, an app with reminders is easier than one long note.

Should the person know I keep a list?

Usually not. The surprise is part of the gift, so keep the list somewhere private. A shared family iPad is not somewhere private.

When should I start?

Today. A list started in October still catches the hints people drop before the holidays, and it’s ready for every birthday after.
